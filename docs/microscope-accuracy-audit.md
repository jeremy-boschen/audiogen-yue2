# Microscope accuracy audit — 2026-09-27

The saved generation is credible, but the explorer is not yet accurate enough to present all of its explanations as established facts. Several statements are demonstrably wrong, and two browser behaviors can associate measurements or listening judgments with the wrong audio.

This is a review, not an implementation change. Application code, existing documentation, recorded runs, and published-build directories were left unchanged.

## Scope and evidence

Reviewed `docs/microscope-explorer.md`, `docs/microscope.md`, the four explorer HTML pages, all four first-party app scripts, the Python data builders, capture/measurement code, score parser, analysis scripts, publish command, and explorer tests. Also inspected the simple listening page generated by `microscope.py`.

Review baseline: audiogen-yue2 commit `74a0eccd8a109de7dde8a0b70a8e280de19ba6b9`. The recorded engine is a different, explicitly identified artifact: YuE commit `1bdbee928be66a109d66ba12d4be62b2f6adf8c9` in the editable `.venv-dev` environment. The run metadata and that historical solver source were checked, rather than assuming today's pinned environment made this run.

The examined run is `../audiogen/output/microscope-runs/20260926-slow_down-s4417-take2/on-cpu`. Both existing builds use it. Their focus/stack HTML and app scripts match the reviewed source byte for byte.

Verified:

- All **108 Python tests pass** using `.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q`.
- Captured and saved studio `abc_tokens.npy`, `semantic.npy`, and `latent.npy` are exactly equal. Score text is also identical.
- The two FLACs decode to exactly equal samples: **4,456,320 stereo frames at 48 kHz**.
- Captured step 32 is exactly equal to `final/latent.npy`. The capture integrity record also reports semantic-stream equality and final-state decoding equality.
- This run has one acoustic chunk, 2,321 semantic/latent frames, 64 latent channels, 32 solver steps, and 33 saved boundary states, covering 92.84 seconds.
- Chromium checks reproduced the replay and Finished-view problems below, with no page exceptions. A deliberately delayed step-audio request reproduced incorrectly attributed listening marks. Annotation requests were intercepted; no real annotations were written.

These checks establish reproduction for this take. They do not establish that every recognizer transcription, instrument label, or scientific interpretation is correct. No new model generation, ASR, separation, R2 upload, or deployment was performed. Browser checks used the existing local build; this was not a full cross-browser or visual-layout audit.

## Findings that should be fixed before presenting the app as an explanation

### 1. The solver is midpoint, not Euler — high confidence

Locations: `explore_assets/app/common.js:275`, `explore_assets/focus.html:22`; supporting engine source `YuE/src/yue2/nar.py`, `CachedNAR.solve`.

Both UI explanations explicitly say “32 Euler steps.” The run's `engine_config.generation.ode_method` is `midpoint`. Its historical solver evaluates velocity at the current state, constructs a half-step state, then uses a second velocity evaluation at that midpoint for the full update. Thus there are 32 midpoint updates and 64 velocity evaluations per acoustic chunk, with 33 saved boundary states. The internal midpoint states are not the 33 displayed checkpoints.

Suggested text: **“This take uses 32 midpoint solver steps, moving from an initial noise latent to the final latent. The dial includes the initial state as step 0.”** The generic ODE glossary formula should either show midpoint explicitly or label its single-velocity formula as a simplified illustration.

### 2. The stack replay is neither real-speed nor a fully observed generation — high confidence

Locations: `explore_assets/stack.html:24`; `explore_assets/app/stack.js:9`, `:27`, `:359–400`, `:424`; `explore.py:480–494`; explorer guide `:96–100`.

The UI promises “replayed at the real speed” and “at the speed it happened.” The implementation compresses the entire animation into about 22 seconds, interpolates between sparse token checkpoints, and assigns the acoustic stage an invented duration of `semEnd * 0.12`. Its animated latent is shader-generated noise blended into the final latent, not the recorded intermediate latent states.

For the actual default run, the score was loaded with `--plan-from .../studio-take`. `abc_observed` is 0, `abc_stream_matches` is null, and every exported plan checkpoint has a null generation timestamp. Plan prefixes are slices of the reused plan, not observations of it being written during this capture. Null times are subsequently used in numeric interpolation; the replay skips the score-writing phase rather than representing a measured history. The visible “written in 1:28.7” is the last semantic checkpoint timestamp, not the duration of the complete generation. The take metadata reports 139.9 seconds for rendering.

Suggested text: **“Accelerated illustration of the generation stages. Semantic-token progress uses recorded checkpoints, interpolated between observations. This take reuses a saved score; its writing was not recorded. The acoustic animation here is illustrative.”** Show the score as supplied/reused, and label the clock “elapsed to final semantic token.” The focus page is where real acoustic checkpoints can be inspected.

### 3. Listening marks can record a step the listener has not heard — high confidence; browser reproduced

Locations: `explore_assets/app/focus.js:430–450`, `:662–665`.

`sound()` keeps the old audio playing until the newly selected step is loaded and decoded. `claim()` nevertheless records `Math.round(ui.target)`, not the audible step. In the reproduction, step 0 was playing, requests for step 10 were delayed, and selecting step 10 then tapping “I hear a voice” saved a step-10 claim while `focusAudio.loadedStep` and the sounding URL still identified step 0.

This affects both browser marks and the local annotations sent to disk. Disable marking while the requested audio is pending/crossfading, or explicitly record the audible source, view, and playback time. Display a loading distinction between selected and audible steps.

### 4. Finished mode displays statistics for a different state — high confidence; browser reproduced

Locations: `explore_assets/app/focus.js:746–782`, `:882–890`.

Finished mode switches the terrain/audio to the final state, but `renderPanel()` still indexes `D.metrics[ui.target]` and chooses the state branch. At step 0, clicking Finished displays “pure static: the start,” `t 1.00`, waveform match **0.109**, latent match **0.437**, and band agreement **0.037**, while playing/showing the finished take. The final-state waveform correlation is 1.0.

Either show final-state statistics in Finished mode or clearly separate “selected checkpoint statistics” from the final reference being played. The terrain tooltip also always reports state-band agreement, including in Predicted mode; label both values explicitly or select the active view.

### 5. The claimed bass-first/high-frequency-last order is contradicted by this run — high confidence

Locations: `explore_assets/focus.html:58`; `explore_assets/app/focus.js:40`.

The app says bass and rhythm usually lock first, fine detail last, and that “air” is the last to clear. Neither is established by one run; the latter contradicts this run's displayed statistic. Grouping bands by geometric center, the median step from which envelope correlation remains at least 0.9 is:

| Frequency group | State | Predicted |
|---|---:|---:|
| 40–250 Hz | 23 | 2 |
| 250–1,000 Hz | 20.5 | 3.5 |
| 1–4 kHz | 22 | 0 |
| 4–10 kHz | 19 | 0 |
| 10–16 kHz | 20 | 0 |

Every predicted band above 4 kHz passes the sustained threshold starting at step 0. This does **not** establish that high-frequency audio is already correct; it shows why the statistic cannot support the narrative currently attached to it.

Suggested text: **“Different bands reach similar rise-and-fall patterns at different steps in this take. The tick marks the first saved step after which correlation stays at or above 0.9.”** Remove the claimed universal ordering. Also, 10–16 kHz is about 0.68 octave, not “the top octave.”

### 6. Correlation and visual effects are being described as identity, certainty, or completion — high confidence

Locations: `explore_assets/app/focus.js:727–729`, `:147–158`, `:208`, `:883–890`; `explore_assets/focus.html:37–44`; `explore.py:60–81`.

- **“Waveform match: 1 is identical, 0 unrelated” is false.** Pearson correlation is 1 for a positive rescaling plus a constant offset, even when the samples differ. Zero means zero linear correlation, not absence of all relationship. A direct call to the implementation with `x` and `3*x + 10` returns 1.0.
- Settling is a **whole-song correlation per frequency band**, repeated along that entire band. It does not detect that a particular instrument, word, or local spot has finished forming. Correlation can remain high despite amplitude differences. The 0.9 cutoff is an app convention, not an established perceptual boundary.
- The displayed average “Band agreement” averages correlations after clipping negatives to zero and quantizing to bytes. It is not precisely the mean of the original signed correlations. Document that transformation, or calculate the displayed statistic before quantization.
- Color and glitter are visualization effects. Settling color even consults the final take's terrain values; it is a retrospective comparison, not the model revealing its confidence. “Gray glitter” is also stale: the shader uses frost teal.
- The Ghost explanation says its gap shows how far the model still has to go. The ghost has an artificial **7-unit vertical lift**, and both surfaces use nonlinear height scaling. Even identical surfaces retain the lift. The gap is not a remaining-error measurement.

Suggested waveform text: **“Similarity of the waveform shape. A value of 1 means perfect positive correlation; it does not prove identical samples.”** Suggested settling text: **“Color shows how similarly each frequency band's level varies over the whole song compared with the final step.”** Explain that the ghost is raised for visibility.

### 7. Flow time and predicted audio are useful approximations, but the text overstates them — high confidence

Locations: `explore_assets/app/common.js:274–291`; `explore_assets/focus.html:15–38`; `explore_assets/app/focus.js:754`; `docs/microscope.md`, “Two views of the ODE.”

`t` is a solver coordinate, not a measured fraction of audible noise. `x_t - t*v(x_t,t)` is correctly implemented as a host-side extrapolation of the current velocity to the endpoint. It is not the eventual result of the remaining nonlinear solve, and it does not establish what the model has “already decided” or “knows.”

The initial Gaussian noise is in **latent space**, then passed through a learned audio decoder. Calling its output “pure static” suggests raw white audio noise, which is not what is being measured. Also, “step 0 is pure static” is especially misleading in Predicted mode: that view includes a model evaluation even at step 0.

The entire acoustic chunk is updated together in this run. However, the score and semantic stages are autoregressive, and the engine supports multiple acoustic chunks. Broad claims that the model “doesn't write a song from start to finish” or always processes the whole song at once erase those distinctions.

Suggested text: **“The acoustic stage updates this take's whole latent sequence together. State decodes the saved latent at a checkpoint. Predicted decodes a one-jump estimate made using the current velocity; it can differ from where later steps end up. Flow time runs from 1 at initialization to 0 at the final step.”** Describe progress percentages as completed solver steps, not percentage of musical completion.

### 8. Frequency, loudness, and brightness descriptions need more precise wording — high confidence

Locations: `explore_assets/app/common.js:287–288`; `explore_assets/app/focus.js:35–40`, `:730`, `:883–887`; `microscope.py:138–177`, `:536–561`.

The terrain is pooled **log10 summed STFT power** of the mono mix, clipped/quantized and nonlinearly displaced. It is not a pitch detector or a calibrated perceptual-loudness measurement. The tooltip's “dB below the loudest point” uses the **99.9th percentile** display reference, not the actual maximum. The glossary says STFT magnitude, although the implementation sums squared magnitudes before taking logs.

Brightness is a magnitude-weighted mean **frequency**, not the average pitch or an energy-weighted pitch estimate. Its help also accidentally includes an unrelated sentence describing band correlation. The implementation's centroid agrees with the conventional magnitude-spectrum definition in the [librosa reference](https://librosa.org/doc/0.11.0/generated/librosa.feature.spectral_centroid.html).

Use **“frequency”** for the depth axis, **“relative band level”** for height, and **“dB relative to the display reference (99.9th percentile)”** for the tooltip. Frequency-band instrument examples can remain as approximate listening guides, with a statement that instruments overlap many bands. Do not imply that 1–4 kHz isolates a voice/melody, that bass necessarily holds most of the energy in every mix, or that noise necessarily disappears last at high frequencies.

### 9. Audio processing makes “exactly as it sounds” too strong — high confidence

Locations: `microscope.py:191–194`, `:304–307`; `explore.py:296–298`, `:369`; `bin/explore.py:114–122`.

Metrics/terrains use raw float WAVs. Focus playback uses separately peak-normalized, PCM-16 listening copies; publishing then encodes those copies as lossy AAC at 160 kb/s. Stack playback instead starts from the unnormalized final WAV. The two pages can therefore play the same final performance at different levels. Audio crossfades and terrain interpolation also create transitions between checkpoints rather than additional recorded solver states.

The existing `audio_note` mentions normalization, but it is buried in a title attribute and does not mention published AAC. Add a visible short note: **“Audio previews are peak-normalized for listening; web previews use lossy AAC. Measurements come from the original decoded audio.”** Avoid presenting cross-step loudness changes as unprocessed model output.

### 10. The score, lyrics, and transcript explanations mix distinct evidence — high confidence

Locations: `explore_assets/app/common.js:281–295`; `explore_assets/app/stack.js:12–23`, `:253–263`, `:316`; `explore.py:197`, `:407–425`, `:462–468`.

- The actual ABC score contains notes, rests, chord symbols, and section labels, **not lyric text**. Lyrics were supplied separately and paired with phrases by analysis. Replace “score: notes, chords and lyrics” with “notes, chords and sections; supplied lyrics are shown alongside.”
- “The key signature is not applied” is stale and false. `score.parse` now applies major/minor signatures and bar accidentals. `K:Cm` produces E-flat, A-flat and B-flat in a synthetic check, and existing published pitches equal the current parser output.
- Notes-per-syllable is a phrase-level ratio of score note onsets to a heuristic syllable count. It does not measure how each sung syllable was allocated to notes, nor how long it was held. Replace “higher is drawn-out singing” with “higher means more written note onsets per estimated lyric syllable.” A long held syllable can contain just one note.
- `fold_pickups()` extends the next phrase's span but deliberately does not add the folded note to its note count. The displayed phrase's ratio then excludes its added pickup; recompute it or disclose the exclusion.
- “Heard” is an ASR estimate, not verified lyrics. Although the help admits recognition errors, “what actually came out,” “no words heard,” and “nothing” are stronger than the evidence. Prefer **“Recognized words (may be wrong)”** and **“No words recognized in this assigned interval.”**
- Phrase assignment uses score timing with a fixed 0.3-second early allowance. It does not align each lyric phrase to the singing. State that explicitly.
- For shorter takes, “the last N seconds of the plan were never sung” is not implied by a duration difference: timing may drift or material may be skipped/compressed. Say **“The score timeline extends N seconds beyond the audio; this does not establish which planned material was performed.”** This message is conditional and is not triggered by the current 92.84-second take.
- Semantic token IDs are categorical codes, not a meaningful numerical scale of voice/instrument/pitch. The stack uses ID magnitude for position, height and color; explain that these are display choices. Latent channels likewise are not frequency bands. “No fine detail” and “this is where the song's character is decided” overstate what is known about their contents.

### 11. Entry cues and the listening game are not measurements of when parts become intelligible — high confidence

Locations: `bin/find_entries.py:55–67`, `:93–135`; `bin/machine_listen.py:138–170`; `explore_assets/app/focus.js:585–596`, `:689–703`.

The documented onset threshold describes the implementation reasonably well, but detected stem activity is not proof of the named instrument or its first audible entrance. The six-stem model's own [Demucs documentation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs#demucs-music-source-separation) specifically warns about piano bleed/artifacts. A whole-song level cutoff cannot establish that a quiet stem is bleed or a loud stem is a real source. The current drums cue was manually moved from 19.8 to 21.9 seconds, which usefully demonstrates the distinction.

Use **“estimated entry cues, with any manual corrections recorded”**, and retain/display provenance when relevant. The first recognized word and first non-N chord are recognizer outputs, not definitive ground truth. Machine-listening thresholds compare to the recognizers' own final-take outputs. In particular, the voice score compares **total detected duration**, not overlapping voice intervals, so “half the finished take's voice” is too strong.

Resolve changes song position and solver step simultaneously. Cues are gated by final-song entry estimates, and only three unmarked cues are shown at once. A later instrument cannot be marked at an early step during that pass, and a fourth cue can remain hidden until another is marked. Consequently the dots record **where the user clicked during this experience**, not the earliest step where each part could be heard. Keep the game, but describe it that way. A controlled comparison would hold the same audio excerpt fixed across steps and allow all relevant judgments.

### 12. Resolve's promised timing is not guaranteed — high confidence

Locations: `explore_assets/app/focus.js:311–315`, `:841–847`, `:906–912`; `explore_assets/app/common.js:100–127`; guide `:79–82`.

The intended pace targets one second before the last score chorus. Actual advancement uses accumulated render-loop `dt`, resets away excess time, and pauses when the tab is hidden while audio can keep playing. Resolve only seeks to the beginning when the selected step is zero; starting it from an intermediate step does not play “from the top.” Loading can also leave the audible step behind the selected step (finding 3).

Describe the timing as an intended demonstration pace, or derive step progression from the audio clock and explicitly define restart/resume behavior. It is not the model's recorded wall-clock generation schedule.

## Take map qualifications

The map correctly discloses that similarity is a signal statistic and that axes have no intrinsic meaning. `project_takes()` implements the documented correlation-derived distance, exponent 0.3, and classical MDS. However:

- Three-dimensional positions approximate the transformed distances; spatial closeness is not an exact reading of the correlation matrix. The numeric nearest-neighbor list is more direct.
- “Seeds scatter / noise clusters / N-step solves trace a path” should describe observations in this dataset, not laws of generation. The connecting curve is drawn through separately generated takes; it is not an observed solver trajectory.
- `MIN_TAKE_SECONDS` is 190 although `MAP_SECONDS` is 200. A 190–199-second take is pooled across its actual duration into the same 400 columns as a 200-second take. This stretches the time comparison and contradicts “at the same times.” Require the full comparison window or pool on a common physical-time grid. This is a code-path finding, not a claim that the current map contains such a take.
- Calling envelopes “phase-free” means STFT phase is discarded after making a mono mix. Stereo cancellation can still change that mix and its magnitude spectrum; it is not invariance to every phase change.

## What can safely remain as the core explanation

The broad sequence—symbolic planning, autoregressive semantic tokens, flow-based acoustic latents, VAE audio decoding—agrees with the [official YuE2 model card](https://huggingface.co/m-a-p/YuE2-3B). The [official VAE card](https://huggingface.co/m-a-p/YuE2-Vae) supports 64-channel latent input and 48 kHz stereo decoding. The local run independently establishes its 25 Hz frame rate and exact output shape. Official current examples do not override this run's recorded fork/profile behavior.

A concise, defensible introduction would be:

> This explorer shows saved intermediate states from the acoustic generation of one YuE2 take. The take reuses a saved score, then generates semantic codes and refines a compressed audio representation over 32 midpoint solver steps. The landscape shows frequency-band levels in audio decoded from those states. Colors compare each band's pattern with the final step; they do not identify instruments or measure musical quality. Predicted is a one-jump endpoint estimate, not a guarantee of what later steps will produce. Listening previews are normalized, and the web version uses AAC.

Prioritize the two audio/measurement attribution bugs, the solver and replay claims, and the unsupported settling narrative. The remaining changes mainly require careful labels, provenance, and explicit limitations rather than replacing the underlying visualization.
